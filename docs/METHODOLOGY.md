# Methodology and governance

## Data

Demo observations are generated by a committed deterministic formula across five labelled occupations, six state/district combinations and twelve months. The fixture is synthetic and not an official statistical estimate. Imports are aggregate CSV only; validation is all-or-nothing, and source label/access method are recorded. The UI/API identify synthetic rows and imported rows distinctly.

## Demand and supply

Demand counts are displayed as source-provided proxy counts. The current fixture has no comparable worker-supply measure; there is no gap classification. Training seats are a separately reported capacity value, not workforce supply. High demand is not treated as a shortage.

## Forecast

Monthly observations are summed and fit to an ordinary least-squares straight-line trend indexed by month. At least three months are required; forecast horizon is one to twelve months. Negative predictions are clipped to zero and rounded to an integer. The model does not estimate uncertainty or claim accuracy. It is a baseline for demonstration, not a validated labour-market forecast.

## Early warning

For each occupation and district, the API compares the latest two observed months. A 25% or larger increase produces a review alert showing both counts and the exact rule. It signals only a change in the synthetic or imported demand proxy. It is not a shortage or oversupply conclusion.

## Allocation

An exact dynamic program selects whole seats by maximizing total assigned seats up to the latest demand proxy, subject to user-entered aggregate budget and district capacity. Seat caps are based on reported training capacity. User-entered cost per seat is an assumption. A minimum seat request permits infeasibility demonstrations. Outputs report cost, count and proxy coverage; there is no causal outcome or placement impact model. Saved scenarios preserve their input and result snapshot.

## Governance

Use aggregate, permitted and privacy-preserving sources only. Do not upload worker personal data. NCO is an occupational classification; NCS is an employment/career service; PLFS is survey-based and not a live vacancy source. No external government integration is present. Human review is required. This local prototype does not claim government security certification, regulatory compliance, or production readiness.
